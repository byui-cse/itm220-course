How to use this reference

Use this while planning a reporting query. You do not need to memorize the schema.

  1. Click a table that seems related to your question. The panel on the right explains what the table represents and shows its exact relationships and join fields.
  2. Ask yourself: What information do I need to show? For example, a customer’s name, a film title, or a rental date.
  3. Use the search box or a focused view, such as Rentals or Purchases & library, to find the other tables you need.
  4. Follow a relationship before writing your query. Match the foreign key to its related key—for example, rental.inventory_id connects to inventory.inventory_id.
  5. Write down the table path first, then build your JOIN statements one connection at a time.
PK Primary key: uniquely identifies one row.
FK Foreign key: points to a row in another table.
PK/FK A key field that is both part of the primary key and a connection.

High-value reporting paths

These are common routes through the schema. They are not the only correct routes, but they are a good place to start.

What did a customer rent?

customer → rental → inventory → film

What did a customer buy?

customer → purchase → purchase_item → film

What is in a customer’s library?

customer → customer_library → film

Who appeared in a film?

film → film_actor → actor

Which category is a film in?

film → film_category → category

Which provider offers a title?

provider → inventory → film

Details that matter

Why is inventory between rentals and films?

An inventory row records that a specific provider offers a specific film. A rental points to that provider-title listing through inventory_id.

Why are there two small “film_…” tables?

film_actor and film_category are junction tables. They let one film have many actors or categories, and let one actor or category connect to many films.

How are purchases different from a library?

purchase is the order, purchase_item is each film on that order, and customer_library is the resulting film access for a customer.
